Five candidate causes were tested and **eliminated**, each with a measurement: the force field (a rigid H-O-H scan is smooth and monotonic across the full range including the 1/sin(theta) point at 180 deg, where the gradient stays at 0.087 Eh/A, and rises correctly to 261.8 Eh/A at 3.6 deg); stale state between steps (`-cleanenergy true`, which rebuilds the calculator every step, makes it **worse** - +173.7 Eh against +53.2 on a 300-atom cluster); a missing pair in a frozen cutoff list (the setup topology gives **bit-identical** forces to a freshly perceived one at the breaking geometry, max|g| 176.1013 Eh/A either way); bond perception (the collapsing water has both O-H bonds, 1.836/1.835 Bohr, indistinguishable from any other); and its electrostatics (1502 neutral fragments, and its EEQ charges O -0.5706 / H +0.2853 are exactly those of every other water). Reaching that geometry costs 120 to 209 kcal/mol for one molecule, i.e. 250 to 430 kT at the 245 K the run was at, so it is thermally unreachable and was driven by the integration; once the angle is below ~30 deg a 0.5 fs step displaces a hydrogen by 1.1 A and no energy bookkeeping survives. **Lowering dt does not remove the mechanism, only its rate**: the same analysis on a dt = 0.25 CSVR run shows 19 healthy frames at a flat 12.6-17.8x the per-atom mean, then frame 20 at 314.8 and an escalation to 4920 - on *different* atoms, and the run still gains +12.3 Eh over 300 fs. **`-adaptive_step_local` (new, on by default when `adaptive_step` is on)** therefore adds a second rejection channel that measures the kinetic energy of the **hottest atom over the per-atom mean**: dimensionless, needing no decomposition of the potential, and almost size-independent because the maximum of N chi-squared samples grows only logarithmically in N. Over the same 60 healthy steps of polymer_2x the total-energy drift has median 48.96 kcal/mol and healthy maximum 190.45 (**max/median 3.89**, so the default factor 5 puts the threshold at only **1.29x** the healthy maximum - that is the documented failure of the global criterion, in one number), while the hottest-atom ratio has median 9.70 and healthy maximum 12.19 (**max/median 1.26**, so the default factor 10 puts the threshold **8x** above it). It is a **pure addition** - it can only reject a step the global criterion accepted - and on the deterministic single-water regression case the trajectories with and without it are **bit-identical to twelve decimals**; on a 300-atom cluster, where the global criterion already works, it fires **zero** times. The local channel deliberately only observes until its median has enough samples, with no fixed ceiling, because a ceiling is not knowable in advance for a run with thermal regions at different temperatures and one a run starts above would reject every step forever. Both running medians and the rejection counters are now cleared in `prepareRun()`, so a reused SimpleMD object (ConfSearch drives many trajectories through one) cannot inherit the previous run's calibration. **Result on the system this started from** - polymer_2x, 7320 atoms, 300 fs NVE at dt = 0.5, same seed and thread count: **+67.59 Eh / 2175.1 K without, +0.53 Eh / 246.2 K with**, 104 of 600 steps redone and **92 of them rejected by the local channel alone**, i.e. the global criterion would have accepted them. The event is damped rather than removed: the hottest-atom ratio reaches 911 instead of 3733 and those atoms hold 24.2 % of the kinetic energy instead of 99.0 %, which is the residual +0.53 Eh. Cost about 1.9x the wall time. The previous entry's claim that the feature "does NOT scale beyond ~1400 atoms" is superseded for the local channel; the section documenting the global channel's failure is kept, because it is the measurement this one was designed from. **Also fixed, and it is what hid all of this**: `m_Etot` was assembled only at print steps while `AverageQuantities()` averages it every step, so `<Etot>` averaged a value hundreds of steps old and the final status line reported an apparently conserved Etot (-906.522666 Eh, bit-identical from 0.5 fs to 300 fs) for a run whose Epot + Ekin had diverged by 428 Eh. `md_adaptive_step` gained a fourth check for the pure-addition property. 🤖 machine-tested.
- **A step-rejecting integrator, so a GFN-FF MD survives dt = 1 fs on a large solvated system (Sep 19, 2026)**: the heating of `polymer_2x` (7320 atoms) was traced in the previous commit to single steps in which a momentarily compressed GFN-FF O-H bond (3817 -> 10758 cm-1 at 0.70 A) pushes the stiffest mode past what a 1 fs velocity-Verlet step can follow; one such step injects energy that the thermostat then spreads over the whole system. The practical answers so far were `-dt 0.25` or `-hydrogen_mass 4` - both pay for a handful of bad steps with every step of the run, and the mass trick changes the dynamics by construction. **`-adaptive_step true`** instead measures the quantity the step is supposed to conserve and, when the step violated it, discards the step and redoes it with a subdivided time step: ordinary numerics, no constraint and no mass modification. **Off by default**, and an explicit `false` is bit-identical to a binary that never had the feature. The conserved quantity is `E_pot + E_kin` with the kinetic energy taken BEFORE the thermostat, and the bath work over the step is measured in `ApplyThermostat()` and subtracted, so the criterion is exact for every thermostat rather than only CSVR. **The threshold is self-calibrating, because a fixed energy cannot work**: the Verlet energy error is a bounded oscillation whose amplitude is the sum over all modes, so the median per-step |dE| at 300 K/1 fs is 0.35 kcal/mol for one water, 6.3 for 40 waters and 195 for a healthy 1410-atom polymer - any fixed number rejects every step of the polymer or none of the water. What IS system-independent is the spread within a run: over all five systems the largest healthy step is 1.7 to 3.4 times the median of the same window, while the step that destroys a trajectory is 1919x resp. 2134x it. So the threshold is `adaptive_step_factor` (default 5) times the running median of the accepted steps, capped at `adaptive_step_tol` (default 1.0) times `N_dof*kB*T/2` for the warm-up. **Only a step accepted on the first attempt calibrates the median** - feeding the subdivided ones back lets a degenerating trajectory raise its own threshold, measured as +1.50 Eh with 89 rejections against -0.01 Eh with 8. **Measured** (200 fs NVE, dt 1.0, fixed seed): 100 waters **+53.26 Eh / 37303 K -> +1.10 Eh / 412 K**, 200 waters **+81.37 / 28710 -> +0.46 / 261**, one water at 1750 K **+8.78 Eh -> -0.006**; and on the three systems that are healthy at 1 fs (1 water, 40 waters, the 1410-atom polymer) the default rejects **nothing** and the trajectory is bit-identical to a run without the feature. The factor is a real dial: tightening it to 2 gains another factor of 5 on a troubled system and costs 22 % of the steps of a healthy 120-atom one, loosening it to 10 or 20 rejects fewer steps and conserves worse. **The size limit, measured and NOT favourable**: on `polymer_2x` itself, the 7320-atom system this whole investigation started from, the feature makes the run **worse** (E_pot at 100 fs -911.08 Eh without against -894.57 with) at ~45 s per step. The reason is the criterion's contrast, and it is not a bug: the legitimate per-step fluctuation is a sum over all modes and grows with the system, while a local defect stays local. Healthy-phase max/median is 3.4 at 120 atoms, 3.3 at 300, 1.7 at 1410 - and **5.94 at 7320**, i.e. wider than the default factor, so the threshold falls inside the normal distribution and about half the steps are rejected at random. The destructive step is 1919x the median at 300 atoms and indistinguishable at 7320. A global energy criterion therefore works up to roughly 1400 atoms (measured) and needs to be replaced by a local one (per atom / bond / fragment, not implemented) beyond that; for polymer_2x there is now **no demonstrated recipe at all**: re-running `-dt 0.5` (the documented one) for **300 fs** instead of 100 gives `<T>` = **716.6 K** against a 300 K setpoint, with the running-average kinetic energy going 9.55 -> 24.91 Eh while the potential only oscillates, i.e. about +8 Eh of total energy gained *while the CSVR thermostat is trying to remove it*. The 100 fs window of the original table simply ends before the effect is visible, and `-dt 0.25` / `-hydrogen_mass 4` were never measured past 100 fs either. **The thermostat is not the cause**: the same case in plain NVE at dt = 0.5 over 300 fs gains **+67.6 Eh** and reaches `<T>` = **2175 K**, with the potential staying flat (-916.3 -> -915.2 Eh) - every bit of it goes into kinetic energy. **And it scales as dt²**: +191 Eh per 200 fs at dt = 1.0 against +45 Eh per 200 fs at dt = 0.5, a ratio of 4.24 against the expected 4, i.e. plain velocity-Verlet truncation error. That fixes the required step by arithmetic rather than by tuning: 300 fs below ~4 Eh on this system needs dt ~ 0.125 fs. The table in the document is now flagged accordingly. New ctest `md_adaptive_step` reproduces the failure mode on THREE atoms (one water, 1750 K, NVE) and checks all of: off by default, explicit false identical, on conserves. Calibration tables and what it does not do in [docs/MD_LARGE_SYSTEMS.md](docs/MD_LARGE_SYSTEMS.md). **Also corrected in that document**: it claimed an EEQ runaway-charge guard had been added; the guard was measured, found to break GMTKN55 (MAD 0.198, max 166 kcal/mol - `IL16/229` legitimately carries 4.63 e) and reverted, and the text now says so. 🤖 machine-tested.

- **Periodic container wall + termination reason (Sep 2026)**: `wall_potential = pbc` is a third wall behaviour next to harmonic and logfermi. It exerts NO force: `SimpleMD::wrapIntoContainer()` moves a whole fragment that left the container back in on the opposite side (per axis for a box, to the antipodal point for a sphere), so unlike the two potentials it does not do work on an escaping molecule and does not heat the system. Whole fragments are translated by one common vector, never single atoms, because tearing a molecule across the boundary would stretch its bonds to container size and the reactive scan would then break the bond. **Deliberately incomplete**: the energy has no minimum-image convention, so a wrapped fragment does not continue its interactions across the boundary and its destination can be occupied (measured on 2 N2 + 6 H2 at 3000 K in a 4.5 A sphere: a wrap landed 0.024 A from another atom and the run turned NaN one step later). Until the non-bonded terms are periodic, a wrap into occupied space is refused and the fragment is reflected elastically instead, which conserves kinetic energy exactly. Contained and free of heating, but not periodic — the real fix is scoped in `docs/WP-PERIODIC-NONBONDED.md`. Measured confinement over 20 ps in a 4.5 A sphere, largest final distance from the origin: harmonic 11.00 A, logfermi 8.76 A, pbc 4.98 A.
