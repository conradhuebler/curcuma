- **The MD time step was 1.9516x too large, for every method, since always (Sep 19, 2026)**: SimpleMD integrates in Angstrom/amu/Hartree. Velocities are drawn as `sqrt(kb_Eh*T/m)`, i.e. in sqrt(Eh/amu), and the position update is `x[A] += m_dT*v - 0.5*g[Eh/A]*(1/m)[1/amu]*m_dT^2` - so `m_dT` must carry the unit `sqrt(amu*A^2/Eh) = 1.9516144 fs`, not one femtosecond. Both terms demand the same unit, so the integrator was internally CONSISTENT: energies, forces, temperature and energy conservation were all correct, and that is exactly why it survived for years. Only the clock was wrong - `-dt 1.0` integrated 1.95 fs and `-MaxTime 300` covered 586 fs. **Found by tying the MD clock to something that does not come from MD**: the Hessian frequencies, validated against xtb 6.7.1 to 0.13 % (Known Issue #28). Stretch one O-H by 0.006 A and read the oscillation period off the trajectory - GFN-FF 4.6961 fs against the 9.1745 fs its own 3635.7 cm^-1 demands (ratio 1.9536), GFN2 4.6861 vs 9.1454 (1.9516), GFN1 4.5803 vs 8.9385 (1.9515); a small-amplitude H-O-H bend gives 1.9527 independently. **Method-independent by construction** - the integrator receives a gradient in Eh/A through `ComputationalMethod` and never sees the method - so ORCA, TBLite, XTB, Ulysses, EHT, PM3 and QMDFF were affected identically, as were ConfSearch's exploration MD and `polymerbuild`. Fixed by converting in the three places where a user time meets the integrator (`Verlet()`, `Rattle()`, `NoseHover()`) with `CurcumaUnit::Constants::FS_TO_MD_TIME`; everything that merely counts time (`m_currentStep`, `m_maxtime`, the `m_coupling/m_dT` ratios in Berendsen/CSVR, print/dump cadence, `rm_COM`, the MTD deposit stride, COLVAR, restart) stays in real fs and is now correct as a consequence. `m_dt2` removed - the squared step is derived locally so the two cannot drift apart. After the fix the same periods come out at -0.09 %, +0.01 % and +0.02 %. Energy conservation unchanged and still scaling with the step (water NVE 500 fs, GFN-FF +1.29e-05 / +2.55e-06 / -1.66e-07 Eh at dt = 0.5/0.25/0.125). **This reframes the large-system MD investigation**: the GFN-FF O-H period is 9.17 fs, so the runs that blew up at `-dt 1.0` were sampling it 4.7 times per period against the ~20 an unconstrained X-H bond needs; `-dt 0.25` reached 18.8 and behaved, and `-hydrogen_mass 4` worked by the same arithmetic. - **A step-rejecting integrator, so a GFN-FF MD survives dt = 1 fs on a large solvated system (Sep 19, 2026)**: the heating of `polymer_2x` (7320 atoms) was traced in the previous commit to single steps in which a momentarily compressed GFN-FF O-H bond (3817 -> 10758 cm-1 at 0.70 A) pushes the stiffest mode past what a 1 fs velocity-Verlet step can follow; one such step injects energy that the thermostat then spreads over the whole system. The practical answers so far were `-dt 0.25` or `-hydrogen_mass 4` - both pay for a handful of bad steps with every step of the run, and the mass trick changes the dynamics by construction. **`-adaptive_step true`** instead measures the quantity the step is supposed to conserve and, when the step violated it, discards the step and redoes it with a subdivided time step: ordinary numerics, no constraint and no mass modification. **Off by default**, and an explicit `false` is bit-identical to a binary that never had the feature. The conserved quantity is `E_pot + E_kin` with the kinetic energy taken BEFORE the thermostat, and the bath work over the step is measured in `ApplyThermostat()` and subtracted, so the criterion is exact for every thermostat rather than only CSVR. **The threshold is self-calibrating, because a fixed energy cannot work**: the Verlet energy error is a bounded oscillation whose amplitude is the sum over all modes, so the median per-step |dE| at 300 K/1 fs is 0.35 kcal/mol for one water, 6.3 for 40 waters and 195 for a healthy 1410-atom polymer - any fixed number rejects every step of the polymer or none of the water. What IS system-independent is the spread within a run: over all five systems the largest healthy step is 1.7 to 3.4 times the median of the same window, while the step that destroys a trajectory is 1919x resp. 2134x it. So the threshold is `adaptive_step_factor` (default 5) times the running median of the accepted steps, capped at `adaptive_step_tol` (default 1.0) times `N_dof*kB*T/2` for the warm-up. **Only a step accepted on the first attempt calibrates the median** - feeding the subdivided ones back lets a degenerating trajectory raise its own threshold, measured as +1.50 Eh with 89 rejections against -0.01 Eh with 8. **Measured** (200 fs NVE, dt 1.0, fixed seed): 100 waters **+53.26 Eh / 37303 K -> +1.10 Eh / 412 K**, 200 waters **+81.37 / 28710 -> +0.46 / 261**, one water at 1750 K **+8.78 Eh -> -0.006**; and on the three systems that are healthy at 1 fs (1 water, 40 waters, the 1410-atom polymer) the default rejects **nothing** and the trajectory is bit-identical to a run without the feature. The factor is a real dial: tightening it to 2 gains another factor of 5 on a troubled system and costs 22 % of the steps of a healthy 120-atom one, loosening it to 10 or 20 rejects fewer steps and conserves worse. **The size limit, measured and NOT favourable**: on `polymer_2x` itself, the 7320-atom system this whole investigation started from, the feature makes the run **worse** (E_pot at 100 fs -911.08 Eh without against -894.57 with) at ~45 s per step. The reason is the criterion's contrast, and it is not a bug: the legitimate per-step fluctuation is a sum over all modes and grows with the system, while a local defect stays local. Healthy-phase max/median is 3.4 at 120 atoms, 3.3 at 300, 1.7 at 1410 - and **5.94 at 7320**, i.e. wider than the default factor, so the threshold falls inside the normal distribution and about half the steps are rejected at random. The destructive step is 1919x the median at 300 atoms and indistinguishable at 7320. A global energy criterion therefore works up to roughly 1400 atoms (measured) and needs to be replaced by a local one (per atom / bond / fragment, not implemented) beyond that; for polymer_2x there is now **no demonstrated recipe at all**: re-running `-dt 0.5` (the documented one) for **300 fs** instead of 100 gives `<T>` = **716.6 K** against a 300 K setpoint, with the running-average kinetic energy going 9.55 -> 24.91 Eh while the potential only oscillates, i.e. about +8 Eh of total energy gained *while the CSVR thermostat is trying to remove it*. The 100 fs window of the original table simply ends before the effect is visible, and `-dt 0.25` / `-hydrogen_mass 4` were never measured past 100 fs either. **The thermostat is not the cause**: the same case in plain NVE at dt = 0.5 over 300 fs gains **+67.6 Eh** and reaches `<T>` = **2175 K**, with the potential staying flat (-916.3 -> -915.2 Eh) - every bit of it goes into kinetic energy. **And it scales as dt²**: +191 Eh per 200 fs at dt = 1.0 against +45 Eh per 200 fs at dt = 0.5, a ratio of 4.24 against the expected 4, i.e. plain velocity-Verlet truncation error.
